A month on the road

25 November 2026

I have not written here since October, and a great deal has changed. This is a short account of what the game does now that it did not do then, without the parts I would rather you found for yourself.

Levels, and a sixth stat

Renown is now the hero’s experience. There are nine levels, and each one gives three stars to spend on any stat you choose. Every class has its own name for every level: the mage begins as a Spellcaster and ends, a long way down the road, as a Grand Sage. The sixth stat is Vigour. It is how hard you are to wear down: more life, a bigger pack, a better night’s sleep, and shorter poison, rot and cold.

What you carry

The ledger has grown to more than five hundred items, and every one now has its own painted icon. Weapons and armour answer to the stats that suit them: a hammer to Might and Vigour, a dagger to Agility and Wits. A good piece in the wrong hands is only a good piece.

A larder and a workshop

Thirty-nine raw materials, from venison and honeycomb to deer hide and iron, can be gathered or bought. At a fire you cook meals and brew tonics. At a smithy or a tanner’s bench you make armour and blades. Three rare materials, Deepsilver, Blackglass and Vaelwood, can only be worked in the region where they are found. Roads now have tinkers and cooks who will lend you their bench or their fire, and a long night can be spent at a camp of your own making.

Who walks beside you

The companions have their own stories now, and some will leave you if you are cruel. One rule is firm: no companion fights for you. They open doors, change what a scene offers and speak when you cannot. The only ones who act in a battle are the two familiars, the Phoenix and Ansiel, because they are not company so much as part of the hero.

The bestiary

There are now 208 foes on the road. I will be honest about it: 48 of them have their own painting, and the rest wear a stand-in until they earn one, and each has an encounter of its own. Charm has its own uses too. A trained voice can hold a creature’s attention long enough to talk first, and six primers teach you the tongues of goblins, elves, trees, giants, dragons and thieves.

A new coat of paint

The buttons, the close marks, the map controls and the life and sanity cells have been redrawn in the same pixel style as the rest, so they no longer look like they belong to a different game.

What is still ahead

Act III is not written. A great deal of testing and balance work is still to come, and there is still no public demo. When there is, it will appear here first.
